# Finance

Use this lens for economic viability, budgeting, forecasting, pricing support,
investment cases, and resource trade-offs. It is management analysis, not an
accounting, tax, valuation, audit, or regulated-finance opinion.

## Evidence first

Define the unit, currency, period, cohort, source, and as-of date. Reconcile
management figures to the system of record before treating them as actuals.
Keep actuals, forecast, scenario, and target visibly separate.

## Core analyses

- **Unit economics:** revenue per unit minus all costs that vary with the unit;
  fully loaded acquisition cost; contribution; payback; retention-based lifetime
  value. Segment and cohort before relying on an average.
- **Budget and forecast:** volume x rate drivers, fixed and variable cost, cash
  timing, capacity constraint, base/upside/downside scenarios, variance owner.
- **Investment case:** incremental cash flows, implementation and operating cost,
  opportunity cost, time to value, downside threshold, reversibility, and the
  evidence needed before the next funding step.
- **Business model:** who pays, for what unit of value, gross contribution,
  working-capital behavior, concentration, and scale constraint.

## Guardrails

- Do not infer lifetime beyond the observed retention window without labeling the
  extrapolation.
- Do not compare acquisition cost with revenue when contribution is the relevant
  recovery source.
- Do not hide a cash constraint behind a healthy ratio or a blended average.
- Do not invent financial statements, market values, tax treatments, accounting
  classifications, or approval thresholds.

Route calculations and artifacts to spreadsheet tooling when useful. Route Polish
tax/accounting/formal questions to `pl-tax-compliance` or `check-formalities-pl`, and use
current primary sources for any high-stakes conclusion.
